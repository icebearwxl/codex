Daily life · Finding a rhythm

Living in Germany.

The things that appear small—housing vocabulary, a Sunday without groceries, a yellow recycling bag—become the texture of living here.

Housing Shopping Recycling Internet Healthcare Daily life

Housing

A room is not only a room.

For a WG (shared flat), clarify the Warmmiete (rent including some running costs), deposit, notice period, internet and whether you can register your address. Never transfer money before seeing credible proof that the offer and landlord are real. Studentenwerk residences are often affordable but may have waiting lists—apply early.

TermMeaning
KaltmieteBase rent, before utilities/heating.
WarmmieteBase rent plus listed running costs; electricity/internet may still be separate.
KautionDeposit; understand the payment and return conditions in the contract.

Shopping

Learn one supermarket, then the rest is easier.

Discounter shops are usually cheaper; opening hours are shorter than in many Chinese cities, and most shops close on Sundays. Bring a bag, check whether produce is priced by piece or weight, and keep a Pfand-friendly bottle habit from the first week.

Recycling

Waste is sorted, not guessed.

Bottles marked Pfand have a deposit, returned at supermarket machines. Paper, packaging, residual waste and often organic waste have separate bins. These are not tests; ask a neighbour or flatmate once and then it becomes routine.

Internet

Home internet takes longer than the SIM card.

In a WG, ask who holds the contract and how the password is shared. If you need your own line, compare installation wait times, contract length and whether the building already has a provider. A generous mobile hotspot is a useful bridge in the first weeks.

Healthcare

Your insurance card is only the start.

Find a Hausarzt after you have insurance confirmation. For non-emergencies, call or book rather than walking in. Pharmacies (Apotheke) can advise on over-the-counter medicine; keep your insurance details in your wallet and on your phone.

Daily life

Quiet Sundays, loud letters, small repetitions.

Most shops close on Sundays. Letters still matter. Neighbours may expect quieter evenings. None of this is meant to intimidate you — it is the operating system. Pair this page with German language, university and jobs when you are ready for the next layer.
