03

Life &
study.

The things that appear small—housing vocabulary, exam registration, a Sunday without groceries—become the texture of living here.

03.1

HOUSING

A room is not only
a room.

For a WG (shared flat), clarify the Warmmiete (rent including some running costs), deposit, notice period, internet and whether you can register your address. Never transfer money before seeing credible proof that the offer and landlord are real. Studentenwerk residences are often affordable but may have waiting lists—apply early.

TERMMEANING
KaltmieteBase rent, before utilities/heating.
WarmmieteBase rent plus listed running costs; electricity/internet may still be separate.
KautionDeposit; understand the payment and return conditions in the contract.
03.2

UNIVERSITY

Read the rules before
the semester accelerates.

German universities expect self-organisation. Course registration, exam registration and deregistration may be separate systems with separate deadlines. Download the examination regulations and module handbook for your programme; they are more useful than guessing from other students’ experience.

  • Use the university email address and check it regularly.
  • Register for exams yourself; attending the course may not be enough.
  • Ask Fachschaft (student representatives) when an administrative instruction is unclear.
  • Start assignments early: libraries and supervisors work on their own schedules.
03.3

LANGUAGE & WORK

German grows through
small repetitions.

You do not need perfect German to begin living in German. Learn the phrases that unlock a conversation: “Could you repeat that slowly?”, “I have an appointment”, “I received this letter”. A weekly language tandem, campus group or part-time job adds repetition that classes alone may not.

For student jobs, start where the structure is kind.

HiWi roles, campus jobs and working-student positions can offer manageable hours and a first German reference. Check your residence status and current work permissions before signing anything.

Daily-life realities

Most shops close on Sundays. Bottles marked Pfand have a deposit, returned at supermarket machines. Waste is sorted. These are not tests; ask a neighbour or flatmate once and then it becomes routine.
