Work that fits a semester

Jobs, with permission first.

For student jobs, start where the structure is kind. Check your residence status and current work permissions before signing anything.

Minijob Werkstudent Translation Teaching Messe / events Job search

Minijob

Small hours, real paperwork.

A Minijob is a capped, often flexible entry point — cafés, campus roles, shops. Confirm the current earnings limit, tax treatment and how it sits with your visa. Keep contracts and payslips with your other Germany documents.

Werkstudent

Start where the structure is kind.

HiWi roles, campus jobs and working-student positions can offer manageable hours and a first German reference. They are usually closer to your subject than weekend shifts, and they teach how German workplaces communicate.

Translation

Chinese–German–English is a skill, not a hobby.

Informal help for classmates is different from paid translation. If you take paid work, agree scope, deadline and rate in writing, and be careful with official or certified documents — those often require sworn translators.

Teaching

Language tutoring can be a gentle first job.

Chinese tutoring, homework help or language exchange with a fee can fit around lectures. Be clear about in-person versus online, cancellation rules, and whether the arrangement is a hobby, Minijob or invoice work.

Messe / event jobs

Short bursts, useful German, tired feet.

Trade fairs and campus events hire hostesses, builders and logistics help. The German is practical, the hours are irregular, and you still need the same permission check as any other job.

Job search

A realistic way to begin.

University job boards, Fachschaft chats, local cafés and a simple German CV are enough to start. Apply with a short, specific message. Do not pay anyone to “guarantee” a student job.

Hours and visa rules can change.

Use the current guidance from your Ausländerbehörde and university before you accept a contract.
